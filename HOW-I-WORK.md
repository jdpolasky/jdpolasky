# How I work with businesses

Most businesses know they need AI but have no idea where to start. I come in, look at your whole operation, and tell you where AI fits and what to do first. Then I bring in the right people to build it.

I start with the business because AI speeds up whatever it touches. If a process is broken, AI makes it break faster. So the first job is finding the real bottlenecks, and the second job is deciding where AI helps.

## Who this is for

Owners and leaders of small and mid-sized businesses who know AI matters and haven't started, or started and got lost. You have a real business with real revenue, and you want it done right.

It's also for businesses that need senior operating help for part of the week. A growing company often can't justify a full-time senior operator, but it can use that experience a few days a month.

## How it starts

A 20-minute call. No pitch. I ask four questions:

1. How would you describe the problem, in your own words?
2. When does it get important enough to spend money on?
3. What have you already tried?
4. What happens if it never gets solved?

You leave the call with a starting point, whether or not we work together.

## What you get

**The map.** I look at how the business actually runs: how work comes in, who touches it, where it waits, and where people spend hours on things a machine could draft. Then you get a plain-English map of where AI belongs, where it doesn't, and what to do first.

**The people.** I don't do large technical builds or system migrations myself. I bring in the right specialists for each part and direct the work, so the pieces fit your business instead of the other way around.

**Someone who uses this stuff.** I run my own work on an AI system I built myself. You can see it in the repos on [my GitHub](https://github.com/jdpolasky). I know what these tools do well, what they get wrong, and what it takes to keep them running.

## A sample map

The business below is invented, to show what a map looks like. The names and numbers are made up.

> **Northfield Supply** sells plumbing and HVAC parts to contractors from two counter locations, with 18 people.
>
> **The problem, in the owner's words:** "My counter people spend half the morning answering the same questions by phone and text."
>
> **What we found:** Most calls are stock checks and order status. Inventory lives in the point-of-sale system, but order status lives in one person's email. Quotes are typed from scratch every time.
>
> **Where AI fits:**
> - Draft quote replies from the parts catalog and past quotes, for a person to check and send.
> - Answer stock and order-status questions by text, once order status moves out of one inbox and into the system.
>
> **Where it doesn't, yet:** Ordering from suppliers. It depends on judgment calls about which contractors pay on time, and that knowledge isn't written down anywhere.
>
> **What to do first:** Move order status into the point-of-sale system. It's cheap, it takes a few weeks, and nothing else on the list works without it.
