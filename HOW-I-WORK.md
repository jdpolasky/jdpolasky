# How I work with businesses

Most businesses know they need AI but have no idea where to start. I come in, look at your whole operation, and tell you where AI fits and what to do first. Then I bring in the right people to build it.

I start with the business because AI speeds up whatever it touches, so a broken process breaks faster. I find the bottlenecks first, then decide where AI helps.

## Who this is for

Owners and leaders of small businesses who know AI matters and haven't started, or started and got lost. You have revenue, and you're ready to put some of it into getting this right.

It's also for businesses that need senior operating help for part of the week. A growing company often can't justify a full-time senior operator, but it can use that level of experience part-time.

## How it starts

A 20-minute call. No pitch. I ask four questions:

1. How would you describe the problem, in your own words?
2. When does it get important enough to spend money on?
3. What have you already tried?
4. What happens if it never gets solved?

You leave the call with a starting point.

## What you get

First, a map. I look at how the business runs: how work comes in, who touches it, where it waits, and where people spend hours on things a machine could draft. You get a map of where AI belongs, where it doesn't, and what to do first.

Then the people. I don't do large technical builds or system migrations myself. I bring in the right specialists for each part and direct the work, so the pieces fit your business.

And someone who uses this every day. I run my own work on an AI system I built myself, and the public version is in the repos on [my GitHub](https://github.com/jdpolasky). I know what these tools do well, what they get wrong, and what it takes to keep them running.

## A sample map

The business below is made up, to show what a map looks like.

> **Northfield Supply** sells plumbing and HVAC parts to contractors from two counter locations, with 18 people.
>
> **The problem, in the owner's words:** "My counter people spend half the morning answering the same questions by phone and text."
>
> **What we found:** Most calls are stock checks and order status. Inventory lives in the point-of-sale system, but order status lives in one person's email. Quotes are typed from scratch every time.
>
> **Where AI fits:**
> - Draft quote replies from the parts catalog and past quotes, for a person to check and send.
> - Answer stock and order-status questions by text, once order status moves out of one inbox and into the system.
>
> **Where it doesn't, yet:** Extending credit to contractors. It depends on judgment about who pays on time, and that isn't written down anywhere.
>
> **What to do first:** Move order status into the point-of-sale system. Answering order-status questions by text depends on it.
